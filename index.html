Ideal Daily Schedule

Wake at 6:30 AM every day — that's the anchor. A fixed wake time matters more than which hour you pick. Bedtime 9:30 PM, nine hours back.

6:30 – 6:45 AM
Up, water, and 10–15 min outside in natural light. The first hour after waking is when light resets the circadian clock — this one habit does more for sleep quality than almost anything else here. Quick face rinse, moisturizer, sunscreen.
6:45 – 7:15 AM
Breakfast, protein-forward. Lighter on cardio days, fuller on lift days.
7:30 – 9:00 AM
Cardio days: train now — beats the August heat and rides the natural morning alertness rise.
10:00 – 11:00 AM
Lift days: train later. Body temperature and the testosterone-to-cortisol ratio both improve through the morning, so late morning is a genuine edge for strength.
After training
Cardio and rest days: cold shower plus a real face cleanse. Lift days: warm shower and cleanse, no cold shower — cold right after lifting blunts the muscle-building signal. Protein and carbs within the hour either way.
Evening
Dinner → read 30–60 min (this is the screen-free wind-down) → PM face routine 30–45 min before bed → journal 5–10 min → screens off → room at 65°F → 9:30 PM lights out.

Wash workout clothes after each use and change the pillowcase weekly — small things that matter at this sweat volume.

The 7-Day Cycle

Built so each day sets up the next: heavy lifting is never back-to-back, leg-heavy cardio never stacks on leg-heavy lifting, and the long cardio day lands when you're freshest. Repeat roughly three times across Aug 2–21.

Day 1 — Heavy full-body liftfully recovered
Day 2 — Easy bike, 20+ kmactive recovery
Day 3 — Trail run + upper bodylower-impact legs
Day 4 — Rest / active recoverybefore lift #2
Day 5 — Heavy lift #2progressive overload
Day 6 — Long cardiofreshest, push distance
Day 7 — Recover + food prepcloses the loop

Cold showers land on days 2, 4 and 6 of each cycle — never right after the two lift days.

Nutrition Timing
  • Protein at every meal — eggs, yogurt, chicken, powder. Spreading it beats loading it all at dinner.
  • Post-workout: protein and carbs within an hour or two. A smoothie with banana works well, and it matters most after the two lift days.
  • Long cardio day: extra carbs that day and the evening before — this is when your body needs the most fuel.
  • Rest days: protein stays the same, total calories can drop slightly.
  • Creatine: 3–5g daily. Timing doesn't matter, consistency does. Drink more water than usual — creatine plus heavy sweating raises hydration needs.
  • Protein target: roughly 0.8–1g per pound of bodyweight per day. That's the one number worth actually hitting.
  • Calories: no counting. Eat until satisfied, never skip meals, add a bit extra on the two lift days and the long cardio day.

As a novice lifter you can build muscle and lose fat at the same time — eating around maintenance, protein-forward, with this much daily movement covers both goals without weighing food.

Everyday rule stays no refined carbs and no added sugar. The post-workout smoothie is the one intentional exception, and that's when fast carbs are actually useful.

Sleep, Optimized
  • Temperature: 60–67°F, with 65°F the sweet spot most sleep researchers point to. A cooler room lets core temperature drop the way deep sleep needs.
  • Sleepwear: light and breathable, or none — it helps the body cool instead of fighting the room.
  • Position: side or back beats stomach for spine alignment. Side sleepers put a pillow between the knees, back sleepers under them. Either way the head pillow keeps the neck level with the spine.
  • Consistent wake time — every day, rest days included — is the single biggest lever, more than bedtime itself.
  • Wind-down: screens off 45–60 min before bed, room cooled ahead of time rather than adjusted after lights-out.
Skincare & Acne

Morning: cleanser → oil-free moisturizer (yes, even with oily skin — skipping it makes acne worse) → mineral SPF 30+, non-negotiable given the outdoor training.

Evening: cleanse, always after sweating → adapalene → moisturizer.

Phase adapalene in so you don't over-irritate:

  1. Week 1 — cleanser, moisturizer, SPF only
  2. Week 2 — adapalene every other night
  3. Week 3 — nightly, if skin is tolerating it

Body: 4% benzoyl peroxide wash on chest, back and shoulders during the post-training shower. Let it sit 1–2 minutes before rinsing — that contact time is what does the work.

Hair: gentle sulfate-free shampoo and conditioner every training-day wash. Daily washing is fine here; the risk is a harsh shampoo, not the frequency. On a recovery day a water rinse alone gives the scalp a break.

Give it 6–8 weeks before judging results — longer than this 20-day block. Expect to build the habit and see early improvement, not a full transformation by Aug 21. Painful or cystic breakouts are a case for a dermatologist, not more products.
